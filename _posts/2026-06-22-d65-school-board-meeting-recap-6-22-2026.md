## 6/22 BOARD MEETING RECAP:

### PUBLIC COMMENTS:

[D65 teacher and parent Beth Brady gave an incredible public comment on what equity is, and what it isn’t.](https://www.youtube.com/live/aj2vaR6N21s?si=y-B5SWjb_zWW68Uh&t=4206) To quote her: “It is dishonest to frame the decisions ahead of us as though we all have an equal amount at stake in a district in which some groups of children continue to experience worse outcomes than other groups, and that these statistical predispositions correlate with those most impacted by historical injustices of redlining and school closures. While each neighborhood is beloved and important, it is dishonest because some neighborhoods were not historically sheltered from budgeting decisions like those we face today at the expense of others.” 

She goes on: “Equity acknowledges and seeks to address current injustices. We are not starting from scratch. We are standing on the shoulders of generations before us who have faced challenging decisions, and we have the benefit of hindsight to see patterns in injustices and how those play out in our community today. So now when the next round of painful decisions need to be made, we can commit not to shoulder the cost equally but to intentionally shoulder the cost in such a way that protects those that have been disproportionately harmed by our decisions up until this point."

[D65 parent Meleah Geertsma](https://www.youtube.com/live/aj2vaR6N21s?si=RjMn5yXB6cCILEdl&t=4427) followed up on her public comment about the displacement of Black residents from the Haven/Kingsley site and spoke about potential civil rights violations at play in our current D65 Board decisions. She said the board has "ethical as well as legal obligations to recognize this much and take corrective action. The Board should fully investigate this history and its legal and ethical duties." 

She goes on: "My research into this history is ... in case the evidence that I am collecting indicates that there is a potential violation of these laws. The board can avoid such a complaint and do the right thing starting today by researching, recognizing and formally apologizing for the harm it caused to the Black and other residents of the Haven and Kingsley site, including the intergenerational harm perpetuated across the district. How to further remedy that harm should be taken up next."

Laurice Bell and several speakers from Shorefront Legacy Center spoke about the mural inside Heritage Hall in Foster school and contract disputes. This came up later in the meeting and the Board agreed that the full $45k should be paid to Shorefront for their work.

[D65 parent Kelly Farley](https://www.youtube.com/live/aj2vaR6N21s?si=KholnZHZG2Gh1c68&t=6168) spoke about how the district is not failing all children \-- but specifically students of color, students with disabilities and low-income students. She said: "These opportunity gaps are a civil rights issue and our response has been to spend years making plans and getting creative on buildings while cutting the very people to who serve students without a clear connection to how we will maintain and improve the student support. We are projecting to be at 65% of our capacity for 2027, we need to find $10 million more in recurring operating cuts in the next 3 years, we have over $200 million in maintenance costs projected for the next 7 years that beg for a capital referendum." She goes on: "We cannot ask for another referendum to cover what we refuse to fix in our structural deficit. We need to show we know what we are doing, hold measurable goals, and get to work."

[I gave a public comment questioning why the Board has not had a meeting to debrief on SDRP](https://www.youtube.com/live/aj2vaR6N21s?si=kloGGVkdjBstiF7v&t=6345) and why if all of the data is in their hands, why we are creating a whole new plan like Elevate (which was shared at the last meeting). I said: "We now have the Master Facilities plan, the special ed audit and the HR audit in hand. The board should look at each of these, analyze them and then make the decisions that the community has been asking for."

[Terri Shepard gave a public comment calling out the fact that we have very recently lost four D65 Black leaders, 3 of whom are women.](https://www.youtube.com/live/aj2vaR6N21s?si=xtjk-zJZ0d6cf1pz&t=6523) She also said: "I would like to address the plan that Maria has offered and say publicly that this plan reminds me of Trump's concept of a plan \-- nothing there but she is asking another group of taxpayers to support another round of consultants to lead us in discussions that we have already had but are hoping for another outcome \-- one that suits them."

THE FUTURE OF BESSIE RHODES AND KINGSLEY was discussed and ultimately the Board agreed to sell the park/green space to Skokie Park District and then go with a closed bid process to another public entity for the building site of Bessie Rhodes. For Kingsley, it was a 5-1 vote to sell to a public entity as well, with Andrew casting the vote to retain it as a swing school. There was much discussion about how to learn more about and honor the history of the Kingsley site, but [when Andrew asked to open an RFP, it was tabled to be discussed later.](https://www.youtube.com/live/aj2vaR6N21s?si=K2lQWf69Pl5PMviK&t=8655) We should continue to push to examine this history and displacement of Black residents.

IN RESPONSE TO THE ELEVATE PLAN PRESENTED AT THE LAST MEETING, ANDREW PRESENTED AN ALTERNATE PLAN: [Stabilize Imagine Plan Invest.](https://www.youtube.com/live/aj2vaR6N21s?si=TfpNC_nf9LBxGwFf&t=8789)  You can read the full plan [here](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1JtqdOwThaeuMgNXjZw6EPubAvI_7zbFp/view). In describing it he said: "We have invested countless hours in SAPs, SDRP, and through this process we have learned difficult but important truths about our finances, our facilities, our staffing, and the long-term sustainability of our district. We have also heard clearly from families, educators, staff, and residents about what matters most \-- equitable, excellent schools, transparency and accountability, a compelling educational vision for D65, strong and supported educators, and a district that can thrive for years to come. We know what the challenges are; we have a lot of data; my proposal is not just a plan to cut costs or reduce buildings \-- it's trying to create a road map for moving D65 from a period of financial uncertainty to a future defined by stability, vision and investment, from short-term reactivity to long-term stability. This plan recognizes that difficult decisions need to be made now but they have to serve a larger purpose and should send us into a transformative direction." 

This plan calls out the need to stabilize district finances first through facility and administration right-sizing, budget reductions, and stronger board policies regarding governing and financial accountability. Then in FY27 the Board would imagine together and create a vision for education excellence. At the start of FY28, they would look at a long-term capital asset plan (something like Elevate). Finally, after all the work has been done, the Board would pursue a capital referendum in late 2028/29. Elevate also calls for a referendum but asks for the money before the budget stabilization has happened.

The framework of what sets Stabilize Imagine Plan Invest apart differs from Elevate in that it follows these principles: 

* Stabilize the system before redesigning it  
* Demonstrate financial discipline before requesting new investment  
* Define a shared strategic plan and educational vision for equitable excellence before redesigning our physical footprint and committing capital  
* Invest only after accountability and planning are established

All offered support for the plan; however, Pat, Chris and Maria don't think we need to phase the various steps that are outlined. Maria states: "The ways that you proposed developing trust with the community through financial sustainability is very important. I also agree with that methodology. The difference is that I don't believe we should close another school without a capital asset plan for all of our buildings. I think you and I just agree on how we should approach this, but I think generally the difference between what you and I are saying here is not that different." Nichole asked Andrew and Maria to come together and combine these plans to bring to the Board.

THE RIGHT AT SCHOOL CONTRACT for FY27 was discussed and after much debate, the Board opted to raise the rates for RAS before and after care in order to get an extra $20k payment from them. Maria did some quick math and said rates would go up about $500/year for 5-day care. The vote to raise rates passed 4-2.

THE BOARD APPOINTED A NEW CFO Eric Miller, as Tamara Mitchell departs at the end of this month, and they announced a mutual separation agreement with Superintendent Dr. Turner, ending her contract a year early, effective June 30th. The Round Table has reported that D65 will be paying her out $191k. 

[When announcing the separation agreement Andrew said](https://www.youtube.com/live/aj2vaR6N21s?si=eb4qurUGldwj_1d_&t=13242): "This is the first all-female, primarily minoritized, leadership team in the history of the district, and I just wanted to celebrate that. They had the audacity to lead this district in its first equitable district-wide school closure process, the first in our history, the first time in decades that we attempted to address the fundamentals of our structural deficits instead of delaying hard decisions and papering over it with more money."

He went on: "I have to sit and imagine if we had sat here and taken your recommendation, Dr. Turner, we would be sitting here having ensured 3-5 years of financial sustainability for D65. We would be sat here having created enough breathing room to fully shift to envisioning, planning and investing. Instead we know how it played out \-- we delayed, we backed off financial targets, we wavered, we deadlocked.

"It's simple math. Our actions have led to this moment ...This could be the last time in a generation that we have an all-female leadership team. We did this and I think we have to own where we stand as a board."

RACIAL EQUITY TRAINING was suggested, especially for the white members of the board, and apparently the Board is not in compliance on these trainings. The mutual separation agreement was passed 5-1, with Andrew voting against it.

[DR. TURNER GAVE A MOVING GOODBYE](https://www.youtube.com/live/aj2vaR6N21s?si=y7-tVDBd0nJOCzoD&t=13913) celebrating the accomplishments of her and the team she built in her three years with D65. She thanked Sergio, Mya and Andrew specifically of the current board members. Dr. Eric Witherspoon, who served as ETHS District 202 Superintendent from 2006-2002, was named as Interim Superintendent, a role in which he can serve for 3-4 months before a new Superintendent will need to be named.
