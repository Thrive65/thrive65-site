Closing Lincolnwood and Dewey would raise K-5 building utilization from about **62%** this year to **76%** in fall 2027, using the district's capacity method. The increase comes almost entirely from removing seats, not from changes in enrollment:

| K-5 schools (excluding King Arts), district method | 2026-27 | 2027-28 (proposed) | Change |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Enrollment (excluding STEP and RISE) | \~3,160 | 3,101 | −2% |
| Capacity (seats) | \~5,130 | 4,098 | −20% |
| Utilization | \~62% | 75.7% | \+14 points |

The district's Board resolution uses 75% K-5 utilization as its test, and the plan clears it by less than one point. Several receiving schools would run noticeably fuller: Willard rises from 53% to 88%, Walker to 83% and Oakton to 81%.

## **Average class size**

General education classes would average roughly **20-23 students** in fall 2027, up from **about 19** this year. Two-way immersion (TWI) classes average about 16 and African-Centered Curriculum (ACC) classes about 12 (ACC sometimes combines grades, which means fewer, larger classes), the same both years, because each program strand needs its own class at every grade even when it is not full. STEP's small self-contained classes are left out of all three averages.

| Lincoln (TWI from Dewey next year) | 20 | 17-23 | 17 (next year) |  | 13-18 \+ 6 |
| Walker | 21 | 20-24 |  |  | 15-18 |

* **General education classes fill up most.** Closing two schools removes partly filled neighborhood classes, so the low end of the range rises from about 18 to 20\. Dawes, Oakton and Orrington would run near 23\.  
* **Program classes stay small, and that sets the floor on cost.** TWI averages about 16 across 7 strands (42 classes), from about 13 at Oakton to about 19 at Dawes. Oakton's ACC strand averages about 12 if it runs one class per grade, or closer to 18-24 if it combines grades (for example, 3-4 multi-grade classes); its TWI strand averages about 13\.  
* **Washington is the outlier.** Its two TWI strands use 12 homerooms, leaving about 165 neighborhood students for as few as 7 classes; neighborhood classes could average as low as 14 if it keeps one per grade.  
* **Willard and Walker carry the most uncertainty.** Their neighborhood enrollment sits just above a full strand, so averages depend on whether the school adds an extra class at every grade (lower) or only where needed (higher).

*How we estimated this:* each TWI or ACC strand \= one class per grade (6 classes). Program enrollments for TWI are 2026-27 actual enrollment on the district’s data site. Both are held constant next year. General education \= school enrollment minus program and STEP students. Its range runs from whole strands (one more class at every grade) to the fewest classes needed at the contract guideline average of 24\. 2026-27 general education averages use actual enrollment by grade minus each school's TWI, ACC, and STEP students, spread evenly across grades, with classes capped at the contract guidelines (23 in K-2, 25 in grades 3-5). ACC may combine grades, which would mean fewer, larger classes than shown. Grades are assumed equal in size; actual sections are set grade by grade, and program sizes may have shifted since the SDRP table.

## **How capacity is counted**

The district counts each building's capacity two ways and uses the lower one:

1. **By room size:** classroom square footage at 48 sq ft per kindergartner and 36 sq ft per student in grades 1-8.  
2. **By classroom count:** classrooms times the teachers' contract class-size guidelines (23 in K-2, 25 in grades 3-5, 28 in grades 6-8; 24 is used as the K-5 average).

It removes one room per building for art, and removes STEP and RISE classrooms (plus a clinician room) and their students. For this round it also began counting every classroom over 550 sq ft, rather than only rooms in multiples of six, which adds seats and lowers utilization. The district notes that programs such as two-way immersion and the African-Centered Curriculum need their own classrooms, so a building can run short of rooms before its utilization rate looks high.

## **Questions to ask**

1. Many in the community have expressed concern about being rezoned from a school they can see from their house to a school further away. Given that no school is at the 90% mark, wouldn't it be feasible to have fewer students move unnecessarily? Could the same be true for families whose middle school will otherwise change too?  
2. How many classrooms will STEP, RISE, TWI and ACC use at each school in fall 2027?  
3. At higher utilized schools, will music, small-group intervention and special education services (resource rooms, speech, OT) still have dedicated rooms or move to shared spaces? The capacity count reserves only one room per building, for art.  
4. How many students moving have IEPs? Has consideration been made as to the impact of changing schools, especially for those whose current school is not closing or for those close to middle school who would change feeder patterns?
