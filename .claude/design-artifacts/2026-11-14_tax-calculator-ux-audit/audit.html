What would this cost my household?

A review of the tax impact estimator at wethrive65.org/tax-calculator: layout and hierarchy, WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility, and interface copy, with recommendations for the six areas you raised.

Reviewed 14 November 2026 against the live site. Source: tax-calculator.md, assets/js/tax-calculator.js.

Checked

  • Chrome desktop, 1440px wide
  • Phone width (390px), rendered in a frame
  • Element positions, scroll widths and tap-target sizes, measured in the page
  • Text contrast computed from live colors
  • Focus and announcements after Edit, run by script

Not checked

  • A real screen reader (VoiceOver, NVDA)
  • Spanish (?lang=es-MX) layout
  • Dark mode and high contrast
  • 200% zoom, and a physical phone
  • The renter flow, beyond a read of its copy

The short version

The estimator started as five separate calculators, and the combined view was added on top. Five visible symptoms trace back to that structure. The picker and the forms aren't visibly connected. Edit sends people away from the combined view. On/off toggles sit apart from the pieces they control. Scenario buttons load values into forms the person can't see. And the answer is far from the inputs that change it.

Issues 1, 2 and 3 have one fix: make the combined view the page, and let each piece open in place. Issues 4, 5 and 6 can each ship separately.

What already works: the copy is careful, plain and well sourced. Contrast passes everywhere I measured. Compact buttons already have 44px hit areas, and focus rings are strong. The “How that number is built” trace is excellent and worth keeping as it is.

Your six focus areas

1

The picker looks like options, but behaves like tabs

Must fix

The five large cards switch which form, result, notes, assumptions and “needs a vote” section the page shows. They are styled as option cards (aria-pressed buttons), and the form they control starts 210px below them, after a callout. Nothing visually ties the selection to the panel. At 1440px the cards wrap four plus one, which leaves “Operating referendum” on its own row and makes it look like an afterthought.

The copy calls them “tabs” (“open any tab to change its settings”), but they look like option cards and aren't announced as tabs, so the copy and the control disagree.

Recommendation

Drop the five-way picker and make “All together” the page. Viewing one piece becomes a filter instead of a separate mode: turn the other pieces off (see 3), or use a “Show only this” link inside a piece. When only one piece is on, the result label becomes that piece's name, for example “Capital referendum, added cost in 2030”. That covers the reason the picker exists, a simple starting point, without creating a second way to navigate.

If you want to keep a starting choice, keep it as a one-time question at the top (“What are you trying to figure out?”) that sets which pieces start switched on. It shouldn't stay as a control that permanently swaps the page.

Interim fix if the restructure has to wait: make it a real tab strip attached to the top edge of the “Your numbers” card. Use role="tablist" with arrow-key movement, aria-controls pointing to the card, and the selected tab sharing the card's border. Move #modeNote inside the card, below the tabs.

2

Edit jumps away from the combined view and changes what the headline means

Must fix

Here is what I saw on the live page when I clicked Edit next to Capital referendum:

  • The whole page switches to the Capital view. The piece list disappears, and the scroll position stays put, so you land at the bottom of the bond form instead of its first field.
  • Keyboard focus falls to <body>, so a keyboard or screen-reader user starts over from the top of the page (WCAG 2.4.3).
  • The headline drops from $1,157 / year to $138 / year, and the only cue is a small eyebrow label. Someone glancing at the result would think the change cut their cost by 88%.
  • There's no “back to all together” control. You have to scroll up to the picker to return.

Recommendation

Make each row in #stackSummary a disclosure that opens that piece's fields in place. Keep “Your numbers” (own/rent, EAV or tax) always visible above the list. Move the piece-specific notes, assumptions and “needs a vote” text into the piece's own panel.

Show each piece's dollar amount for the spotlight year in the row's value column (the B–E figures from the trace). The list then works as a live breakdown of the headline, and opening a piece's settings never changes what the headline means.

Implementation notes

  • The bond fields are shared. HLS and Capital use the same #borrowBlock inputs, swapped by saveBond()/loadBond(). For both to be editable in place, each needs its own field set. The cleaner route is to render one bond fieldset per bond piece with prefixed ids, and drop the save/load swap.
  • renderStackSummary() rebuilds the list's innerHTML on every render. That would wipe any inputs placed inside it, and lose focus on every keystroke. The rows need to become static markup in the page, with JS updating only each row's note and amount text.
  • Use the existing .accordion block for each panel. Adding name="piece" to the <details> elements makes it an exclusive accordion (one open at a time) with no extra JS, if you want that.
3

“Include in the total” is separate from the pieces it controls

Should fix

The four toggle pills sit above the scenario section and the piece list, so the same four names appear twice, about 400px apart, with different controls. When a piece is off, the only sign in the list is “(left out)” appended to its name, which is easy to miss.

Recommendation

Put a real <input type="checkbox"> at the start of each row, as in the sketch above, and delete #stackPieces. An excluded row shows its state three ways, not by dimming alone: the label reads “Not included”, the swatch turns to an outline, and the amount becomes a dash. Its settings stay editable.

Keep the checkbox outside the <summary>. An interactive control nested inside a summary has an unreliable role and click target across browsers and screen readers. Lay the row out as a grid of [checkbox] [disclosure button], with the checkbox labelled by the piece name.

4

A one-button scenario picker, with a note that always shows

Should fix

“Community v4 package” is the only choice, so it reads as a switch, but it can't be switched off. It loads silently on page open (DEFAULT_SCEN), and its pressed state disappears without explanation as soon as you change any referendum amount. Its 85-word note shows whether or not the scenario is active, and still says “This button loads…”.

Recommendation

Use a labelled <select>. Its options come from SCEN, plus a final Custom option that is selected automatically whenever scenarioMatch() fails. Picking Custom by hand keeps the current values.

Referendum amounts Community v4 package (Nov 2026) ▾

Show the note only for the selected scenario. Store it as SCEN[k].note and generate the options from the same object, so adding a proposal is still a single entry and no longer needs markup edits as well. For Custom, show: “You've changed the referendum amounts. Choose a scenario to start over from it.”

Place it directly above the Capital and Operating rows it affects, or label it so the scope is clear: “Referendum amounts (capital and operating)”.

5

The result is out of sight while you change things

Must fix

The headline result card starts 2,640px below the picker on desktop and about four phone screens down. Every change (a scenario, a piece, a rate) means scrolling down to check and back up to adjust. That cuts against what the page is for: seeing what happens when you change something.

Phone: a slim sticky bottom bar.

  • Dock only the headline: the year amount, per month, and the spotlight-year control. The full 467px card stays in place as the detailed view. Docking all of it would cover half a laptop screen.
  • On phones, hide the bar while the full result card is on screen (an IntersectionObserver), and turn docking off when the viewport is short (max-height: 500px) or text size is 150% or more. Otherwise it eats the space people need to type.
  • The dock must never cover a focused field (WCAG 2.2's “focus not obscured”). Add scroll-padding-bottom equal to the bar's height.
  • The existing #resAnnounce live region already announces the result after a short delay. Don't make the dock a second live region.
  • The breakpoint goes through @custom-media, so check the docking in Firefox with the flag on, or in a production build.

The wide tables scroll sideways inside the cards, and on phones that hides every number

At 390px wide only the “Borrowing” label column shows. Every dollar figure is off to the right, and there's no shadow or other cue that the table scrolls. Neither scroll container can take keyboard focus, so keyboard users can't scroll them at all (WCAG 2.1.1).

Recommendation

Make the tables need less width before breaking them out. A table that pokes out past its card's border looks like a layout bug. And at 78rem the wide measure is still narrower than these tables' content on a 1280px laptop.

  • #chartTable: the width comes from the long column headers. Use short headers with the matching chart swatch (Levy · HLS · Capital · Operating · Total), keep the full name in an abbr or visually-hidden span, and add font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums. Six short numeric columns fit in 41rem. On phones, make the first column sticky.
  • #finTable: on screens at --sm-and-down, stack each borrowing as its own block of label and value rows, reusing the .steps layout already on the page. On desktop, shorten the headers (“Yearly payment”, “Interest, year 1”, “Total interest”) and consider moving “Interest, average per year” into the note below.
  • If a breakout is still needed afterwards, end the card before the table and put the table in its own .wide section with a matching heading. Don't stretch it out of the card.

Accessibility fixes to make either way: give each .levy-table-wrap tabindex="0", role="region" and an aria-label. Make the first cell of each body row <th scope="row"> (it's a <td> now). Add a <caption>.

Suggested order

1
Quick fixes, no redesign: table scroll regions and row headers, the shortened chart-table headers, unique Edit labels with focus moved into the form, and the copy changes.
2
The scenario select (issue 4). Self-contained, and it gets new proposals ready to add.
3
The restructure (issues 1–3 together): static piece rows with checkboxes and in-place panels, a field set for each bond piece, and the five-way picker retired.
4
Docked result (issue 5). Easier once the inputs are a single column of pieces.
5
Stacked borrowing table on phones (issue 6), if the shorter headers aren't enough.
