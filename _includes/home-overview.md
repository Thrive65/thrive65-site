The Coalition

## Who we are

Thrive65 is a community-based coalition dedicated to supporting a strong, equitable, and financially sustainable future for all students in District 65\. We bring together parents, caregivers, educators, community members, and neighbors who believe that equitable, excellent schools require bold vision, expert implementation, and responsible stewardship.

We gather the district's own records and outside reporting into one place and translate it into plain language, so you can understand what's happening and advocate for what you need. We share our own positions too, always clearly marked, so you can use the facts whether or not you agree with us.

## The current situation

* Enrollment down **25%** since 2018, a steeper drop than any nearby district   
* Foster opens and Kingsley and Bessie Rhodes close. District buildings are projected to be less than **70%** full in the coming school year.  
* Aging buildings with roughly **$410 million** of repairs due by **2037**  
* **$10 million** in additional operating cuts required by **2030**  
* Struggling to maintain 90 days cash on hand — **bottom 5%** of school districts statewide  
* 4 departures from District leadership in 2026,, including the Superintendent, CFO, Assistant Superintendent of Instructional Leadership, and Director of Schools Management  
* New Interim Superintendent and CFO started in July. Interim Superintendent only here through December 2026\. 


## What We Believe

### Equitable Excellence

Every student deserves access to an equitable, excellent education that addresses the unique needs of each individual student and the communities they represent. We believe that equity and excellence are not competing goals but mutually supportive commitments. By focusing on great teaching, strong programs, and the whole-child development of every student, we can make sure each of our schools is an environment where all children can thrive and reach their full potential.

### Financial Responsibility

To thrive, our district needs maintained facilities, a strategically aligned budget, and stable reserves. Without it, we can’t choose how to cultivate our kids, we just react to whatever crisis comes first. We need a budget that is balanced operationally and structurally- that means the increases in revenue or decreases in expenses to balance it are replicable each year. One-time influxes of revenue, refunds, or credits, are not acceptable in a truly balanced budget; those should be additive. A budget is not balanced if it reduces our annual commitment to building maintenance to avoid a loss.

Financial health is a foundation for thriving students.

### Humanizing and Valuing Our Educators

Uncertainty has a cost, and our educators have carried it alongside us for years. Any real plan must treat the people doing the work with clarity, respect, and fairness. This includes teachers, support staff, administrators, and school employees. Their expertise, dedication, and well-being must be valued and supported. D65 needs policies and practices that attract, develop, and retain exceptional educators through respect, collaboration, and professional growth. When educators are empowered and supported, students benefit from stronger relationships, more engaging learning experiences, and better outcomes.

Empowered and equipped educators are a foundation for thriving students.
